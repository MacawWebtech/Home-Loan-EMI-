First home

The first-time buyer's checklist, from token to keys

Meera Sethi, CFP29 Aug 2026 · 9 min read

Buying your first home involves about forty separate payments and documents. Miss one and your disbursement waits. This checklist puts them in the order you'll meet them.

1. Before you look at a single flat

Work out your budget from your income, not from the brochure. As a rule, keep your total EMIs under half your take-home pay. Then add 8–10% of the property price for stamp duty, registration and interiors.

Check your CIBIL score now. If it's under 750, fixing it first can save you 0.5% on your rate, which is worth more than most negotiations.

"The cheapest money in a home purchase is your own down payment. Every extra lakh you put in saves about two lakh in interest."

2. Booking the property

Before paying the token amount, ask the builder for the RERA registration, the approved plan and the list of banks that have approved the project. A project approved by many banks has usually passed basic legal checks.

3. Applying for the loan

Apply after you have the sale agreement, not before. Banks sanction loans against a specific property. You'll need the documents below.

  • PAN, Aadhaar and address proof
  • Last 6 months of bank statements
  • 3 salary slips and 2 years of Form 16 (or 3 years of ITRs if self-employed)
  • Sale agreement and builder's allotment letter

4. Sanction to disbursement

The sanction letter is a promise, not money. The bank will now do a legal check on the title and a technical valuation. Only then will it release the funds, directly to the seller or builder.

Read the sanction letter for the LTV, the final rate and any conditions. If the valuation comes in lower than the price, you'll need to fund the gap yourself.
